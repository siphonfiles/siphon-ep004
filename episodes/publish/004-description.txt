A sealed box. Plastic tight, seal intact. Inside, a genuine Ledger hardware wallet with the real secure chip and the real software. On 9 October 2026, hundreds of wallets set up on devices like it were emptied within about an hour. One on-chain count puts the total near $102 million from 335 wallets on six networks. Nobody broke the chip. Something behind the screen read the recovery words and sent them out over the mobile network.

This is the story of an extra board hidden where a strip of padding should be, the reseller that changed hands in March, the thief who changed the locks before stealing, and the one address that showed up again five weeks later.

STATUS AS OF LATE NOVEMBER 2026: no public arrest and no charges that we could find. Ledger has not confirmed how many devices were tampered with or how much was lost in total; the figures in this video are Bitquery's on-chain count. Ledger says every loss it has confirmed in the October incident involved devices sold through the reseller CryptoBilis, and that tampered devices found outside Southeast Asia are part of the same incident (confirmed in Berlin; reports from Dubai were still under investigation in its 19 November update). The former founders of CryptoBilis say they sold the company in March 2026 and deny any involvement. Nobody has publicly said who fitted the implants. Details may have changed after publication.
